Choose the profile for the room, not the sample

Collect a few reference images and identify the specific part you like: a border, a centre panel, a cove or a moulding. Ask the installer to mark that feature on a measured ceiling plan. A profile that looks modest in a product photo can take up a large share of a compact room when repeated around all four sides.

Check how the detail meets the wall, wardrobe, curtain track and ceiling fan. The drawing should show start and stop points, corners and any change in level. If two rooms connect, decide whether the ceiling detail continues through the opening or ends at a clear transition.

  • Reference detail translated into a measured plan

  • Doorways and curtain tracks considered

Set lighting before the POP pattern is fixed

Mark the actual light locations on the same drawing as the POP profile. Confirm whether a light sits inside a recess, beside a moulding or on the main ceiling plane. This avoids discovering late that the selected decorative line crosses a fitting or leaves too little space for its housing.

Ask the electrician and ceiling installer to review the route, cut-out and access for each fitting. Where a driver or connection may need service, show how it can be reached after finishing. Use the fitting manufacturer's instructions for clearances and installation requirements.

Quick check

  • Light locations coordinated with profile
  • Cut-outs checked against selected fittings
  • Maintenance access shown

Approve the finish and the scope

Agree which areas receive POP detail and which remain plain. Clarify the expected surface preparation and paint responsibility, then inspect joints and edges under the room's actual lighting before accepting the finish. Keep changes to the layout in the final drawing so trades use the same revision.

For a clear estimate, provide room dimensions, photos, a marked ceiling sketch and the reference for each profile.
