Turn the reference design into a measured ceiling plan

Choose the parts of a reference that matter to you: a POP border, a central panel, a curved corner, a cove or a simple flat ceiling. Ask the contractor to draw those details against the room's measured length and width. The drawing should show where the profile starts and stops, how corners meet and what happens near a beam, curtain track or doorway.

Review the proposed finished height as well as the shape. A deep border or multiple levels takes more room vertically than a small sample image suggests. Write down the proposed level and any areas that remain plain before approving the layout.

Quick check

  • Room dimensions and finished level recorded
  • Border, corners and transitions drawn
  • Plain and decorative zones clearly marked

Coordinate the cove, central light and electrical work

Mark the selected jhoomar or central fixture position on the same drawing as the POP profile. Confirm how the fixture will be supported and where its wiring and driver can be reached. The electrician and ceiling installer should review the fixture's actual dimensions and fitting instructions before the ceiling is closed.

For a cove, ask for a section detail that shows the ledge depth, light position and where maintenance access remains. A plan view helps compare the shape; a section view explains the height and light effect.

Quick check

  • Fixture size and centre point confirmed
  • Cove detail shown in plan and section
  • Wiring and maintenance access coordinated

Compare POP contractor estimates line by line

Ask each POP ceiling contractor to state the measured area, profile or moulding included, surface preparation, finish, paint responsibility, fixture cut-outs, cleanup and exclusions. A decorative POP border and a complete false ceiling are different scopes; compare the same scope before weighing totals.
